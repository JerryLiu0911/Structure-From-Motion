\section*{1. Brief Theory}
\vspace{-0.3em}
\textbf{The Epipolar constraint :}
For corresponding points $\mathbf{x}$, $\mathbf{x}'$ in two images,
$\mathbf{x}'^{\!\top}F\mathbf{x}=0$, where $F$ is the fundamental matrix. This maps the projection of the ray normal to the plane of the first image to the epipolar line in the second image, on which the corresponding point $\mathbf{x}'$ must lie,
reducing the correspondence search from 2-D to 1-D.

\textbf{The Fundamental matrix :}
$F$ ($3\times3$, rank 2, 7 degrees of freedom) encodes the complete projective geometry between two
uncalibrated views: it maps any point in one image to its epipolar line in the
other, capturing the relative pose and the epipoles through its null spaces.

\textbf{Using RANSAC for estimating $F$ :}
Feature matches can often contain outliers (false correspondences). RANSAC iteratively
samples minimal 8-point subsets (7-DOF) to fit $F$ and counts inliers; the hypothesis
with most inlier support is retained, making the estimate robust to the high
outlier fractions typical in raw nearest-neighbour matches.

\textbf{Why having many matches does not necessarily lead to good reconstruction.}
Despite a high number of matches, geometrically inconsistent matches or matches that lack sufficient parallax can lead to poor $F$ estimation and inaccurate epipolar geometry.
Such cases can include matches that are all clustered in a small region of the image, which may not provide enough parallax for accurate triangulation, or matches that arise from repetitive texture with false correspondences. It is
the quality rather than the sheer count of matches that guarantee geometric diversity or epipolar accuracy.

The bad pair images show the same object in the same position, but from completely different viewpoints, leading to

from RANSAC overfitting rather than correct geometry, as confirmed by the
20.43\,px median error over all 17 filtered matches.

We therefore choose the default settings (ratio\,=\,0.75, threshold\,=\,1.0\,px) for a balance of inlier quality and quantity.

\subsection*{Debugging record}
No major bugs were encountered, but some unexpected results prompted deeper investigation:

\textbf{Ratio\,=\,0.90 gave fewer RANSAC inliers than ratio\,=\,0.75.}
Expected: more filtered matches $\Rightarrow$ more inliers.
Actual: inlier ratio fell from 56\,\% to 10\,\%.
Diagnosis: \texttt{matches\_filtered.png} showed geometrically inconsistent
lines; \texttt{median\_epipolar\_error\_all} rose to 30\,px, confirming a
flood of ambiguous false matches that overwhelmed RANSAC.
Fix: retain ratio\,=\,0.75.

\textbf{ Bad pair: near-zero RANSAC inlier error despite broken geometry.}
RANSAC reported almost 0px inlier error, yet
\texttt{median\_epipolar\_error\_all}$=$20.43\,px.
Diagnosis: 8 inliers fitting a 7-DOF model $\Rightarrow$ RANSAC overfits a
degenerate subset. The all-matches median is the true diagnostic.

\subsection*{Experiment choices}

\textbf{Good pair} (\texttt{test\_pair}): same toy, same session, moderate
baseline; chosen because epipolar lines converge and inlier error is sub-pixel.

\textbf{Bad pair} (\texttt{bad\_pair}): same toy photographed in entirely
different rooms; chosen to show the ratio-test failure when backgrounds differ.

\textbf{Sensitivity:} pair mode only, one variable at a time on
\texttt{test\_pair}.
Surprising finding: the drop from 56\,\% to 10\,\% inlier ratio at
ratio\,=\,0.90 was larger than anticipated --- a modest ratio relaxation
can be catastrophic for RANSAC when it tips the outlier majority.
